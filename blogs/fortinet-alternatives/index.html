8 Best Fortinet Alternatives for Modern Security Teams

Palo Alto, Cisco, Check Point, Sophos, Zscaler, Barracuda, and WatchGuard compared against what each is actually built to solve.

Deepak Wanage · Updated 2026

Key takeaways

  • The right Fortinet alternative depends on the problem being solved: Palo Alto fits premium enforcement, Cisco suits Cisco-heavy environments, Check Point fits policy-driven enterprises, Sophos suits teams that value simplicity and cost.
  • Zscaler fits organizations moving toward SASE and zero trust access. WatchGuard and Barracuda suit distributed branch and remote access needs.
  • Fortinet still leads on price-to-performance and all-in-one branch functionality — teams more often switch for fit, support, or cost than missing features.

The eight alternatives

  • Network Intelligence — combines detection, vulnerability prioritization, and cloud security under one operating model. Trade-off: services-led, not a firewall vendor, so perimeter hardware is still needed.
  • Palo Alto Networks NGFW — strong advanced threat prevention and tool consolidation. Trade-off: steep learning curve, and licensing can be costly or confusing.
  • Cisco Secure Firewall — straightforward management and well-regarded support in data center and hybrid setups. Trade-off: non-Cisco integrations can require specialized skills and added cost.
  • Check Point Quantum — broad threat prevention and robust centralized policy management. Trade-off: administration is split across several interfaces; advanced features add licensing cost.
  • Sophos Firewall — no extra charge for SSL/IPsec VPN or MFA, with centralized cloud management via Sophos Central. Trade-off: limited CLI, and firmware updates are sometimes revoked and replaced.
  • Barracuda CloudGen Firewall — observation mode analyzes traffic before migration; SD-WAN supports broadband-based connectivity. Trade-off: frequent updates can cause inconsistencies, and initial setup can be complex.
  • Zscaler Internet Access — cloud-native design removes the need for traditional VPNs for remote and hybrid users. Trade-off: false positives and limited reporting customization add admin work; legacy apps can struggle with SSL inspection.
  • WatchGuard — well-documented support and easy day-to-day administration, suited to resource-limited teams. Trade-off: limited reporting customization, and some models have few physical ports.

The bottom line

Replacing a firewall is rarely just a hardware decision. Network Intelligence positions itself as a control-plane layer that runs alongside whichever enforcement vendor you choose, addressing the operational drag, audit workload, and triage overload that a firewall swap alone won't fix.

Frequently asked

Is there a single best Fortinet alternative?

No — the right fit depends on your environment. Cisco-heavy shops lean Cisco, SASE-bound teams lean Zscaler, and cost-sensitive teams often land on Sophos.

Why do teams switch away from Fortinet?

Usually fit, support experience, or cost structure, rather than a missing feature — Fortinet remains competitive on price-to-performance.

Does a firewall replacement solve audit and triage overload?

Not by itself. That's typically a separate control-plane problem that sits alongside whichever firewall vendor you choose.
