Top OT Security Companies in 2026

Strong OT security is built around industrial context — passive asset visibility, protocol awareness, segmentation, safe deployment, and resilience. Here's how the leading vendors compare.

Deepak Wanage · April 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Strong OT solutions are built around industrial context: passive asset visibility, protocol awareness, segmentation, safe deployment, and resilience.
  • Dragos, Nozomi Networks, and Claroty are the most purpose-built OT vendors. Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, and Forcepoint offer more value from broader enterprise stacks.
  • Pricing across the category is inconsistent and mostly quote-based.

How we compiled this list

We started from the problems operators actually run into: poor visibility into ICS/SCADA and legacy assets, deployment that disrupts production, weak IT/OT segmentation, unmanaged remote access, and alert fatigue in the SOC. Every vendor below was checked against its own product pages and cross-referenced with peer review platforms — G2, Gartner Peer Insights, and Capterra — then evaluated on operational realities: passive deployment, device and protocol accuracy, actionable alerts, SOC fit, multi-site support, and whether both plant and security teams can actually use it.

The leading OT security companies

  1. Network Intelligence — an IoT/OT security partner offering SOC for OT, maturity assessments (NIST CSF 2.0, C2M2), and ISO 62443-aligned consulting. Strength: service-led execution and legacy compatibility. Trade-off: not a single mainstream product; pricing is custom.
  2. Dragos — asset inventory, threat intelligence, managed OT Watch services, and OT-corrected CVSS prioritization. Strength: comprehensive detection. Trade-off: demanding deployment and high cost; pricing is quote-based.
  3. Nozomi Networks — broad OT/IoT visibility with AI-assisted analysis. Strength: easy UI and reliable support. Trade-off: advanced modules need separate licenses and setup is non-trivial.
  4. Claroty — passive asset discovery across OT, IoT, and medical devices, plus exposure management and secure access. Strength: frequent updates and identity integrations. Trade-off: steep learning curve; works best inside its own ecosystem.
  5. Fortinet — network-centric segmentation, ruggedized firewalls, virtual patching, and secure vendor access. Strength: cost-effective with strong segmentation. Trade-off: recurring high-severity vulnerabilities and complex configuration.
  6. CrowdStrike — Falcon for XIoT, unifying IT and OT visibility through a cloud-native platform. Strength: fast deployment and cloud management. Trade-off: false positives, agent resource use, and past stability concerns.
  7. Palo Alto Networks — device identification, IEC 62443-aligned zoning, and segmentation within its platform. Strength: strong visibility and ecosystem integration. Trade-off: can surface more vulnerabilities than teams can action; SIEM integration relies on syslog.
  8. Forcepoint — positioned for secure critical-infrastructure connectivity rather than pure OT monitoring: NGFW, data guard, and one-way transfer. Strength: efficient background operation and good reporting. Trade-off: interface usability and slow historical searches.

Picking the best fit for your organization

Choose on the problem, not the brand: Dragos, Nozomi, and Claroty for OT-native depth; Fortinet and Palo Alto for segmentation, remote access, and stack alignment; Network Intelligence for service-led ownership and maturity building; CrowdStrike for unified IT/OT visibility; and Forcepoint when secure connectivity matters more than passive monitoring.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between OT security tools and OT monitoring tools?

Monitoring tools focus on visibility — surfacing assets, traffic, and anomalies. Security tools add enforcement: segmentation, access control, and response capability on top of that visibility.

What are OT security solutions?

Purpose-built technology and services that protect industrial control systems — SCADA, PLCs, DCS — without disrupting the physical processes they run.

How should buyers compare OT cybersecurity solutions?

On passive deployment safety, protocol and device accuracy, how actionable the alerts are, fit with your existing SOC, and whether the tool holds up across multiple sites.
