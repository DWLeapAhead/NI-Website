CCPA Compliance Checklist: Your 2026 Guide

Consumer rights, the core checklist, and the new cybersecurity audit, risk assessment, and ADMT oversight rules phasing in through 2030.

Aman Pare · Updated 2026

Key takeaways

  • Consumer rights under CCPA include access, deletion, correction, and opting out of data sales or sharing.
  • Data inventory and mapping is the real starting point — everything else builds on knowing what you actually hold.
  • New rules effective 2026–2027 add annual cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and automated decision-making technology (ADMT) oversight.
  • Opt-out mechanisms and Global Privacy Control (GPC) recognition are current enforcement priorities.

Who the CCPA applies to

For-profit businesses meeting any of three thresholds: over $25 million in annual gross revenue, personal information from 50,000+ California residents, or 50%+ of revenue from selling personal information. Consumers gain rights to know, access, delete, and opt out of data sales, plus a right to equal service regardless of whether they exercise those rights.

The checklist

1. Data inventory and mapping: document what personal information is collected, where it flows, its purposes, and retention periods; identify sensitive data specifically.

2. Privacy policies: disclose data categories, sources, purposes, third-party recipients, and consumer rights — updated annually and after material changes.

3. Consumer request procedures: offer multiple submission methods, verify identities, track requests, respond within 45 days (extendable for complex cases), propagate deletions and opt-outs across systems, and train staff on handling.

4. Opt-outs and GPC: a visible "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link, dedicated opt-out pages, honoring GPC browser signals, and blocking third-party sharing for opted-out users.

5. Data security: encryption, multi-factor authentication, role-based access, audits, continuous monitoring, recognized frameworks such as CIS Controls, and breach response procedures.

6. Annual cybersecurity audits (new): required under rules effective January 1, 2026, staggered by revenue — $100M+ by April 1, 2028; $50M–$100M by April 1, 2029; under $50M by April 1, 2030. Independent auditors, gap remediation, five-year report retention, and certification submission to the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA).

7. Risk assessments (new): required for high-risk processing — data sales, sensitive data, ADMT — weighing consumer risk against business benefit, with reports due to the CPPA starting April 1, 2028, and executive attestation.

8. ADMT oversight (new): effective January 1, 2027, requiring qualified human reviewers who can override automated outputs, transparency about decision logic, consumer access where feasible, and protection for trade secrets and fraud controls.

9. Vendor compliance: review contracts for CCPA clauses, confirm vendors can honor deletion and opt-out requests, monitor sharing, and track remediation.

10. Staff training: role-specific training, records, updated materials, and a privacy champion network.

11. Ongoing monitoring: automated compliance tracking, regular internal audits, tracking regulator updates, and documenting everything for audit readiness.

Where programs commonly fail

  • Ignoring or mishandling GPC signals
  • Opt-out processes that are hard to use or not propagated everywhere they need to be
  • Incomplete data inventories that miss shadow IT and legacy data stores
  • Weak vendor contract terms
  • Overlooking the new audit, risk assessment, and ADMT requirements
  • Manipulative design that discourages opt-outs
  • Identity verification that's either too burdensome or too weak

Frequently asked

Does CCPA apply to every business that touches California data?

No — only for-profit businesses meeting at least one of three thresholds: $25M+ annual revenue, data from 50,000+ California residents, or 50%+ of revenue from selling personal information.

What's new for 2026 and beyond?

Annual cybersecurity audits (staggered 2028–2030 by revenue), mandatory risk assessments for high-risk processing starting 2028, and ADMT oversight requirements effective January 2027.

What's a Global Privacy Control signal?

A browser-level opt-out signal that businesses are required to honor automatically, without the consumer having to submit a separate request.
