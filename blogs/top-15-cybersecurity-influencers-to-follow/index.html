Top 15 Cybersecurity Influencers to Follow

Researchers, journalists, and practitioners worth your feed if you want signal ahead of the headlines.

Ashwany Pillai · Updated 2026

Keeping up with cybersecurity means following the people who actually shape the field — researchers, journalists, and practitioners whose work shows up in CVE databases, breach disclosures, and policy debates long before it reaches a headline. Here are 15 worth your feed.

  1. 1. Bruce Schneier — Cryptographer, privacy expert, and author who explains how security affects society.
  2. 2. Eva Galperin — Director of Cybersecurity at the Electronic Frontier Foundation, focused on digital rights and privacy.
  3. 3. Katie Moussouris — Founder of Luta Security and a pioneer of vulnerability disclosure and bug bounty programs.
  4. 4. Brian Krebs — Investigative journalist covering cybercrime and breaches at Krebs on Security.
  5. 5. Mikko Hyppönen — Chief Research Officer at F-Secure, tracking malware, espionage, and global threats.
  6. 6. Troy Hunt — Creator of Have I Been Pwned, and an advocate for data privacy.
  7. 7. Kim Zetter — Award-winning journalist reporting on cyber warfare, espionage, and government surveillance.
  8. 8. Chris Wysopal — Veracode co-founder promoting secure coding and software security education.
  9. 9. Wendy Nather — Advisory CISO at Duo Security, focused on security strategy, risk, and metrics.
  10. 10. Nicole Perlroth — New York Times cybersecurity reporter and author covering cyber conflict and threats.
  11. 11. Paul Asadoorian — Founder and host of Security Weekly, known for security podcasts and discussions.
  12. 12. Robert M. Lee — CEO of Dragos, specializing in industrial control system and critical infrastructure security.
  13. 13. Jack Daniel — Strategist at Tenable who advocates for vulnerability management best practices.
  14. 14. Magda Chelly — Security professional on the Black Hat advisory board, author, and multilingual keynote speaker.
  15. 15. Chris Roberts — Chief Security Strategist focused on threat intelligence and deception technology.

Why it's worth following them

Each brings something different: specialized technical knowledge, early visibility into incidents and policy shifts, educational resources that go deeper than a vendor blog post, and a direct line into the broader security community's current debates. Following a mix — researchers, journalists, and practitioners — gives a more complete picture than any single source.
