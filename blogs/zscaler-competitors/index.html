Top 6 Zscaler Competitors in 2026

Netskope, Palo Alto, Cloudflare, Fortinet, and Check Point compared on what they solve — and where Zscaler users hit friction.

Deepak Wanage · Updated 2026

Key takeaways

  • SASE is mainstream now — Secure Access Service Edge combines networking and security as a standard architecture, not an emerging bet.
  • Vendors are converging SD-WAN and SSE into unified, AI-driven platforms.
  • Zscaler users commonly cite limited flexibility, high licensing costs, complex deployment, and slow support as friction points.
  • Managed SASE is gaining interest from organizations without a large internal SOC or network team.

Six competitors compared

  • Network Intelligence — managed, vendor-agnostic SASE with expert support and AI-driven MDR. Trade-off: doesn't sell DIY software licenses, so it may not suit buyers who want to run the stack in-house.
  • Netskope — broad cloud app coverage with unified SSE and borderless SD-WAN. Trade-off: complex admin console and premium pricing that assumes skilled internal staff.
  • Palo Alto Networks (Prisma SASE) — strong firewall-based security, ZTNA, and DLP with unified management. Trade-off: difficult setup, premium pricing, and reported support challenges.
  • Cloudflare One — fast global edge network, cloud-native design, and a free tier for up to 50 users. Trade-off: deep security inspection is still maturing, with more false positives below enterprise contracts.
  • Fortinet (FortiSASE) — a natural extension for existing Fortinet customers, with bundled hardware, SD-WAN, and SSE. Trade-off: steep learning curve, complex licensing, and limited third-party integrations.
  • Check Point Harmony SASE — user-friendly UI, robust threat prevention, and a single-vendor SASE option. Trade-off: steep initial setup, slower support at times, and some reported tunnel failures.

How to actually choose

Selection should follow your infrastructure, network topology, SOC capacity, and budget — not vendor reputation alone. A lean IT team without in-house SASE expertise usually gets more value from a managed deployment than from the strongest standalone platform run without the staff to tune it.

Frequently asked

Why do teams look for Zscaler alternatives?

Most commonly limited flexibility, licensing cost, deployment complexity, and support responsiveness — not a missing core capability.

Is managed SASE worth it for a smaller team?

Often yes — it avoids the specialist hiring and ongoing tuning that a DIY SASE deployment requires to run well.

Does SASE replace a traditional VPN entirely?

In most modern deployments, yes — SASE converges secure access with networking so a separate VPN layer usually becomes redundant.
