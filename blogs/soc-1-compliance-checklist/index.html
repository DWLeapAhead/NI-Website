Ultimate SOC 1 Compliance Checklist: Your 2026 Guide

The 22-step path from scoping to issuance, plus realistic timelines, costs, and where most organizations get stuck.

Nikita Rane · Updated 2026

What SOC 1 actually covers

SOC 1 is an AICPA standard under SSAE 18, built for service organizations whose services affect clients' financial reporting. Type I tests control design at a single point in time; Type II also tests operating effectiveness over roughly 6–12 months, and carries more weight with clients and auditors as a result.

The standard is built around five control categories: control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring.

Who actually needs it

Payment processors, payroll and loan servicers, claims processors, trust services, managed IT providers, data center operators, and SaaS providers with financial applications — essentially, any service organization whose processing touches a client's financial statements.

SOC 1 vs. SOC 2

SOC 1 targets financial reporting controls specific to the organization, with restricted report distribution. SOC 2 uses standardized Trust Services Criteria — security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy — and is the right fit when the concern is broader operational trust rather than financial statement impact specifically.

The 22-step checklist

Planning and scoping: identify services in scope, identify key financial controls, set control objectives and design, document control descriptions, risk-assess each control, and plan the audit.

Preparation and testing: conduct a readiness assessment, gather evidence of operating effectiveness, test design efficiency, test operational efficiency, document test results, and evaluate exceptions and deficiencies.

Reporting: compile the SOC 1 report, get audit lead approval of the final report, and present the report to management.

Remediation: define corrective actions, monitor them, retest failed controls, get management approval of corrective actions, and revise the report as needed.

Issuance and retention: issue the final report, and retain all documentation for at least five years.

Timelines and costs

A first Type I audit typically runs 6–9 months; a first Type II runs 12–18 months. Type I costs land around $20,000–$40,000 for small organizations, $40,000–$75,000 for mid-size, and $75,000–$150,000+ for large ones. Type II typically runs 25–50% more than Type I.

Where organizations get stuck

Incomplete documentation, insufficient control testing, managing controls across multiple locations, and limited staff awareness of what the audit actually requires are the most common blockers.

The takeaway

SOC 1 compliance is a continuous process, not a once-a-year event. Treating readiness, testing, and remediation as standing practices — rather than a pre-audit scramble — is what separates a smooth Type II renewal from a stressful one.

Frequently asked

How often does a SOC 1 audit need to happen?

Type II reports typically run on an annual cycle to stay current for clients who rely on them for their own audits.

Can our internal team issue the SOC 1 report?

No — it must be issued by an independent, licensed CPA firm acting as the service auditor.

Does SOC 1 overlap with ISO 27001 or NIST?

There's overlap in control areas like access management and monitoring, but SOC 1's scope is specifically financial-reporting controls, so it doesn't substitute for either framework.
