Ultimate SOC 2 Compliance Checklist: Your 2026 Guide

The 10-step path from scoping to report distribution, and the difference between Type I and Type II that actually matters to your customers.

Aman Pare · Updated 2026

Key takeaways

  • SOC 2 reports against five Trust Services Criteria — Security is mandatory, the other four are optional based on scope.
  • Type I reports on control design at a point in time; Type II reports on operating effectiveness over a 3–12 month window, and carries far more weight with enterprise buyers.
  • Most first-time audits take 6–9 months end to end, including remediation time after the gap assessment.

Trust Services Criteria

Security (the "Common Criteria") is required in every SOC 2 report. Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy are added based on what your customers actually need assurance over — a payments platform will usually add Processing Integrity; a healthcare-adjacent vendor often adds Privacy.

Type I vs. Type II

Type I assesses whether controls are designed appropriately as of a single date. Type II assesses whether those controls actually operated effectively over an observation period, typically 3–12 months. Enterprise procurement teams increasingly ask for Type II by default, since it demonstrates sustained operation rather than a snapshot.

The checklist

1. Define scope — which systems, products, and Trust Services Criteria the report will cover.
2. Executive sponsorship — a named owner with budget and authority to drive remediation.
3. Readiness/gap assessment — compare current controls against the chosen criteria.
4. Remediate gaps — prioritize by audit risk and implementation effort.
5. Select an audit firm — a licensed CPA firm; compare experience with your industry and tooling.
6. Choose Type I or Type II — and the observation window if Type II.
7. Collect evidence continuously — screenshots, logs, and policy sign-offs mapped to each control, ideally automated.
8. Run the audit — documentation review, control testing, and interviews with control owners.
9. Review the draft report — resolve any exceptions before the final report is issued.
10. Distribute and maintain — share the report under NDA with customers, and keep evidence collection running for the next cycle.

Common pitfalls

Scope creep (adding criteria you don't actually need), leaving evidence collection as a manual quarterly scramble instead of a continuous process, and underestimating how long remediation takes relative to the audit itself — remediation is usually the longest phase, not the audit.

Frequently asked

Do I need Type I or Type II?

Most enterprise customers expect Type II, since it demonstrates controls operated effectively over months, not just that they were designed well on one date.

How long does a first SOC 2 audit take?

Most first-time audits take 6–9 months end to end, with remediation after the gap assessment usually the longest phase.

Which Trust Services Criteria are required?

Security is mandatory in every SOC 2 report; Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy are added based on what your customers need assurance over.
