Palo Alto Networks Alternatives: Enterprise Firewall Solutions Compared

Fortinet, Cisco, and Check Point compared against Palo Alto on cost, complexity, and what each is actually built to prioritize.

Nupur Swetambari · Updated 2026

Key takeaways

  • Palo Alto is known for threat prevention, application visibility, and centralized management through Panorama — but deployment can be complex and costly once add-on licenses stack up.
  • Modern NGFWs go beyond packet filtering: application awareness, intrusion prevention, threat intelligence, SSL/TLS decryption, centralized management, scalability, and cloud integration are all table stakes now.
  • Evaluate on total cost of ownership, scalability, ease of management, integration with existing tools, and advanced threat prevention — not brand recognition alone.

Four alternatives compared

  • Network Intelligence — offloads firewall management and incident response to a fully managed service. Trade-off: not a traditional hardware firewall vendor, so it suits buyers wanting a service rather than a device.
  • Fortinet (FortiGate series) — high throughput, built-in SD-WAN, and generally lower cost than Palo Alto. Trade-off: its performance-first focus may not suit buyers prioritizing deep application-level visibility.
  • Cisco (Secure Firewall series) — tight integration with the Cisco ecosystem, including ISE, and a well-known enterprise hardware reputation. Trade-off: value is greatest for organizations already invested in Cisco networking.
  • Check Point (Infinity architecture) — strong advanced threat prevention and unified management across the network. Trade-off: the platform-based, multi-layered approach can add complexity for buyers wanting something simpler.

The bottom line

Palo Alto remains a strong fit for large enterprises that need deep application visibility and centralized management. Organizations with tighter budgets, simpler management needs, a preference for high-performance hardware, or existing Cisco infrastructure will often find one of the alternatives above better aligned with their priorities — and a managed service may suit organizations with a small IT team regardless of which firewall vendor they land on.

Frequently asked

Is Palo Alto overkill for a mid-size organization?

It can be — the deep application visibility and centralized management shine most at scale, and the licensing cost compounds quickly below that.

Which alternative is cheapest to run?

Fortinet generally comes in lower on both hardware and licensing, though exact totals depend on throughput and feature tier.

Should we consider a managed service instead of any of these?

If your IT team is small, a managed service can offload both deployment complexity and ongoing tuning regardless of which firewall vendor sits underneath it.
