ISO 27001 vs. SOC 2: Which Does Your Business Actually Need?

A certification against an internationally recognized standard, versus a US-centric attestation report — how to decide, and why many companies end up with both.

Udit Pathak · Updated 2026

Both exist to give customers assurance over how you handle data, but they're structurally different: ISO 27001 is a certification against an information security management system (ISMS); SOC 2 is an attestation report produced by a CPA firm against the AICPA's Trust Services Criteria.

How they compare

  • ISO 27001 — an internationally recognized, certifiable ISMS standard covering a full risk management lifecycle — strong fit for global buyers and regulators. Trade-off: certification requires a formal management system and ongoing surveillance audits, which is a heavier lift to stand up initially.
  • SOC 2 — an attestation report (not a certification) that's faster to obtain and widely expected by US enterprise SaaS buyers specifically. Trade-off: has less international recognition than ISO 27001, and reports are typically only shared under NDA rather than publicly referenced.

Which one do you actually need?

If your buyers are primarily US-based SaaS companies, SOC 2 is usually the expected baseline. If you sell internationally, or into enterprises and governments that reference ISO standards contractually, ISO 27001 carries more weight. Many growing companies eventually pursue both — the underlying control work overlaps substantially, so a second certification is incremental rather than a second ground-up effort.

Where the overlap helps

Access control, risk assessment, incident response, and vendor management controls largely satisfy both frameworks simultaneously. Mapping your control set once against both frameworks, rather than building two parallel programs, is the efficient path if both are on the roadmap.

Frequently asked

Is ISO 27001 a certification and SOC 2 just a report?

Yes — ISO 27001 is a certification issued after an accredited body audits your ISMS; SOC 2 is an attestation report issued by a CPA firm, typically shared only under NDA.

Can one control set satisfy both frameworks?

Largely yes — access control, risk assessment, incident response, and vendor management controls overlap heavily, so mapping once against both frameworks is more efficient than building separate programs.

Which is more recognized outside the US?

ISO 27001, since it's an internationally standardized certification rather than a US-centric attestation format.
