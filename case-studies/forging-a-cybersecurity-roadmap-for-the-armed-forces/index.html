Forging a Cybersecurity Roadmap for the Armed Forces

A three-year cybersecurity roadmap spanning people, process, and technology, built so leadership could see the value, not just the spend.

The challenge

The client was responsible for establishing cybersecurity across a branch of the armed forces and needed a three-year roadmap covering people, processes, and technology. Basic technology and teams were already in place, but without a clear, sequenced direction, the organization struggled to demonstrate cybersecurity's value to senior leadership — making it hard to secure the ongoing investment the mission required.

What a roadmap like this needs to solve

For a defense-sector client, a cybersecurity roadmap isn't just a technology shopping list. It has to sequence investment against mission priorities, build internal capability rather than create permanent vendor dependency, and give leadership a way to see progress in terms they can act on — not just technical maturity scores.

Why this matters beyond defense

The pattern here is common well outside the military: teams with reasonable baseline technology and staff who still can't get sustained investment because there's no roadmap connecting security work to outcomes leadership cares about. A multi-year plan that sequences people, process, and technology investment — and reports progress in business terms — is what turns security from a cost center into a visible, fundable program.
